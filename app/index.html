THE AUTONOMY GAP

Unlimited authority is unsafe.
Per-action approval kills autonomy.

Individuals and companies want AI agents to complete real work and pay for data, APIs, translation, and other services. A blank-check wallet is too risky, while asking a human to approve every action prevents the agent from operating independently.

AgentLedger makes authority earned and enforceable: start small, prove work, gain a larger cap, and lose authority when policy is violated.

PROTOCOL BEHAVIOR

Small rules.
Meaningful boundaries.

MandateGraph is designed to make authority composable without making it wider as it travels.

01

Monotonic delegation

Child budgets, service scope, recipient restrictions, expiry, and depth never widen beyond the parent mandate.

02

Reserved child budgets

Delegation reserves part of a parent’s available budget, keeping sibling work from spending the same dollars twice.

03

Request-bound auth

Each payment ID is bound to task, mandate, recipient, amount, service, resource, expiry, and nonce.

04

Revocation + retry blocking

Ancestor checks prevent revoked authority and its descendants from reaching settlement.

READ THE LABEL

Experimental by design.
Not production custody.

The contract is unaudited. Mainnet work is limited to a controlled proof of concept using demonstration amounts; production deployment remains NO-GO.

The recipient is a separately controlled demo wallet. The lifecycle can demonstrate earned and reduced authority, not an independent vendor relationship, service delivery, or service quality.

Outcome hashes are caller-supplied lineage data; they do not independently attest to offchain work. ERC-8004 provides identity and trust inputs; AgentLedger enforces payment authority.
