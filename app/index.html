THE CONTROL GAP

A spend limit answers “how much.”
It does not answer “why.”

AI teams increasingly need to buy data, translation, APIs, and other services. A wallet cap can limit total outflow, but it cannot show which task authorized a payment, which agent delegated it, or what authority remains.

AgentLedger makes that chain explicit with task-rooted, USDC-denominated mandates.

PROTOCOL BEHAVIOR

Small rules.
Meaningful boundaries.

MandateGraph is designed to make authority composable without making it wider as it travels.

01

Monotonic delegation

Child budgets, service scope, recipient restrictions, expiry, and depth never widen beyond the parent mandate.

02

Reserved child budgets

Delegation reserves part of a parent’s available budget, keeping sibling work from spending the same dollars twice.

03

Request-bound auth

Each payment ID is bound to task, mandate, recipient, amount, service, resource, expiry, and nonce.

04

Revocation + retry blocking

Ancestor checks prevent revoked authority and its descendants from reaching settlement.

READ THE LABEL

Experimental by design.
Honest by default.

The contract is unaudited. The browser simulation is deterministic fixture data with no wallet connection, backend, indexer, or live Arc reads.

The Mainnet recipient was a separately controlled demo wallet. The lifecycle demonstrates deployment, task-bound payment execution, delegation, revocation, and a blocked retry — not an independent vendor relationship, service delivery, or service quality.

Outcome hashes are caller-supplied lineage data; they do not independently attest to offchain work.
